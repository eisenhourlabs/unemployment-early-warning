Duration: how long unemployment lasts

How long unemployment lasts. State UI continuing claims show it weekly; exhaustion and benefit duration come monthly and quarterly from DOL's state UI reports; the household survey and JOLTS hiring add the wider picture. Continuing claims count people on benefits; they rise with more new claims as well as with longer spells.

SA continuing claims rate

How long people stay on state UI benefits. For involuntary unemployment insurance (IUI), this is the public guide to how long claims last (severity).

Each path takes what the series did after the last month before that downturn began and applies it to today's level (unemployment: the same change in points; claims: the same percentage change). A what-if, not a forecast.

The range has been about right overall and a little too narrow since 2021. It is a range for the next 3 months, not a view on a downturn.

26 and 52-week ranges are not shown: in testing they were too narrow (80% bands held only 69% and 67% of outcomes).

Source and method

Plotted: 4-week average (bold), weekly (faint). Source: DOL state UI claims (ETA 539 weekly report and Thursday news release; FRED CCSA for preliminary weeks). Seasonally adjusted continuing claims as a percent of covered workers, weekly, from 1971; matches DOL's insured unemployment rate. Hollow markers: preliminary. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Dotted lines: average SA weekly rate over the 12 months up to each NBER peak (Apr 2000–Mar 2001, Jan–Dec 2007, Mar 2019–Feb 2020) and over 2019. Last data: Nov 14, 2026, preliminary (advance). Check: within 0.05 pp of DOL's published insured unemployment rate in every week since 1971 (DOL rounds to 0.1). Fan = our forecast range for the 4-week average at 13 weeks: in testing (2000–2026, excluding 2020) the darker band held 51% of outcomes and the lighter band 77%; when it missed, outcomes were more often below the range than above it. What-if paths repeat the 2001 and 2008 recessions from today's level; they are not forecasts. Forecast elements appear only when the time range includes today.

Continuing claims per new claim

How many weeks’ worth of new claims are still on the rolls. It rises when people stay on benefits longer, months before the exhaustion rate does.

Our calculation from DOL claims via FRED (CCSA, ICSA): 4-week average continuing claims ÷ average weekly initial claims of the prior 26 weeks. Monthly (last week of each month). Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Nov 2026.

Exhaustion rate (12-month)

Share of claimants who use up all their state benefits. It does not show whether they then found work.

Source and method

DOL standard definition · NSA, 12-month average. Source: DOL ETA 5159 (state UI claims and payments), our calculation: 12-month average final payments ÷ 12-month average first payments 6 months earlier. Percent, US. US = sum of all states (can differ slightly from DOL's published US figure: 0.08 pp in 2026 Q2). Monthly, dated at month end. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.

Average benefit duration

Weeks of state UI benefits paid per claimant, against weeks entitled. It covers people receiving state benefits, not all unemployed people. For involuntary unemployment insurance (IUI), the closest public match to benefit months.

Source and method

DOL standard definition · NSA, 12-month average. Source: DOL ETA 5159 and ETA 218, our calculation. Bold: 12-month weeks compensated ÷ 12-month first payments, all claimants, monthly. Grey: average potential (entitled) duration of new claimants, quarterly. Weeks, US; quarters with a state missing are left blank. Q3 2026 is not shown yet: not reported by Alabama. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: average weeks paid Oct 2026, average weeks entitled Jun 2026.

Job-finding rate

Share of the unemployed who leave unemployment within a month. When it falls, claims last longer; it is the most direct public measure of how fast people get back to work.

Source and method

Source: BLS Current Population Survey via FRED (UNEMPLOY, UEMPLT5), our calculation: 1 − (unemployed next month − newly unemployed next month) ÷ unemployed this month; 3-month average, percent. Leaving unemployment includes leaving the labour force. Vertical line at January 1994: survey redesign; levels before and after are not strictly comparable. Gap: no October 2025 survey. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.
