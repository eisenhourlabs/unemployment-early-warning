Leading indicators: what tends to move first

Indicators often cited as moving before layoffs. In our own tests most gave little or no extra warning beyond jobless claims; the yield curve and financial conditions gave the longest. Two of them are confirmation triggers with a colour (the yield curve and financial conditions); temporary help and the regional surveys are shown for context. The triggers and the early-warning score are on the Outlook page.

Summary

Sorted by how unusual each reading is on the risky side, most unusual first. Signal: the warning-trigger colour where the indicator is one of the triggers (all triggers on the Outlook page).

Yield curve: 10-year minus 3-month

When short rates rise above long rates (below zero), recessions have often followed within about a year.

Source: U.S. Treasury via FRED (T10Y3M). 10-year minus 3-month Treasury constant-maturity yield, percentage points, daily. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Nov 27, 2026.

Temporary help jobs vs. a year ago

Firms usually cut temporary staff before permanent staff.

Source: BLS Current Employment Statistics via FRED (TEMPHELPS). Percent change in temporary help services employment vs. 12 months earlier, seasonally adjusted, monthly. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.

Average weekly hours, manufacturing

Employers usually cut hours before they cut jobs.

Source: BLS Current Employment Statistics via FRED (AWHMAN). Average weekly hours of production and nonsupervisory employees, manufacturing, seasonally adjusted, monthly. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.

Involuntary part-time work vs. a year ago

Employers cut hours before they cut jobs.

Source: BLS Current Population Survey via FRED (LNS12032194). Percent change in the number working part time for economic reasons vs. 12 months earlier; seasonally adjusted, monthly. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Sep 2026.

Building permits vs. a year ago

Housing turns early in the cycle; fewer permits mean less construction work ahead.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau via FRED (PERMIT). Percent change in new private housing units authorized by building permits vs. 12 months earlier; monthly. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.

Regional manufacturing surveys

Early monthly reads on factory activity; below zero means more firms report declines than gains.

Source: Federal Reserve Banks of Philadelphia and New York via FRED (GACDFSA066MSFRBPHI, GACDISA066MSFRBNY). Current general activity diffusion indexes, seasonally adjusted, monthly; Empire State starts Jul 2001. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Nov 2026.

Financial and credit conditions (NFCI)

Broad financial conditions and their credit component; above zero means tighter than average. Tighter credit makes it harder for firms to fund payrolls.

Source: Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago via FRED (NFCI, NFCICREDIT). Zero = average since 1971, positive = tighter. Weekly, week ending Friday. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Nov 20, 2026.

Banks tightening standards on business loans

When banks tighten credit to firms, hiring freezes and layoffs have followed two to four quarters later.

Source and method

Source: Federal Reserve Board, Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey, via FRED (DRTSCILM). Net percentage of domestic banks tightening standards for commercial and industrial loans to large and middle-market firms; quarterly; above zero = more banks tightening than easing. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.
