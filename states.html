Rising states context, not tested

States where new claims have been 20% or more above normal for 3 weeks in a row, or where unemployment (3-month average) is 0.5 point or more above its 12-month low. A watch list, not a signal: these rules have not been tested on history.

    What moves the weighted change

    Pushing it upptsPulling it downpts

    Contribution of a state = its weight × (its claims rate now − a year ago) ÷ the weighted claims rate a year ago, in percentage points; all contributions add up to the weighted % change.

    Breadth: share of jobs in states with new claims up 20%+

    How widespread rising layoffs are: a few states, or many at once.

    Source and method

    Share of covered jobs in the states and DC whose initial claims rate (4-week average) is up 20% or more on the same weeks a year earlier. Each state counts by its covered employment; the download also has the simple share of states. The 20% threshold was fixed before any testing; added to the early-warning score it did not improve it (tested 2026). Year-on-year on unadjusted data: states' seasonal patterns differ and DOL publishes no state seasonal factors, so each week is compared with the same weeks a year earlier. Source: DOL ETA 539 (ar539), our calculation. Weekly from Jan 1996. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Nov 21, 2026.

    Continuing claims rate: weighted vs. national

    Whether unemployment spells are lasting longer where the weights sit.

    The weighted rate is held down by short benefit limits in heavily weighted states: new claimants there are entitled to 23.4 weeks on average, against 23.9 nationally, and 1% of the weight sits in states with fewer than 20 entitled weeks (DOL ETA 218).

    Source and method

    Continued weeks claimed (regular state UI) as a % of covered jobs, 4-week average, not seasonally adjusted; National = the sum of the states and DC. Source: DOL ETA 539 (ar539), our calculation. Weighted view: each state counts in proportion to a fixed set of regional weights this site uses for its own monitoring. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Nov 14, 2026.

    Unemployment vs. 12-month low (Sahm-style): weighted vs. national

    A rise of 0.5 point or more has marked the start of past recessions nationally.

    Source and method

    3-month average of the unemployment rate minus its lowest 3-month average of the previous 12 months, in points (the Sahm rule), for the weighted rate and the national rate. Monthly. Weighted view: each state counts in proportion to a fixed set of regional weights this site uses for its own monitoring. Grey bands: NBER recessions. Last data: Oct 2026.

All year-on-year figures on this page compare the same thing: the claims rate (claims per 100 covered jobs, regular state UI, 4-week average, not seasonally adjusted) with the same weeks a year earlier, for each state, for National (the sum of the states and DC) and for Weighted. "vs. normal": vs. the median of the same weeks in the prior 3 years (Mar 2020 – Jun 2021 left out). "Last 26 wks": new claims, 4-week average (blue), against the same weeks a year earlier (grey). Unemployment rate: BLS LAUS, seasonally adjusted; "vs. 12-mo low": 3-month average minus its lowest 3-month average of the previous 12 months, in points. Exhaustion and average weeks paid: 12-month averages from DOL ETA 5159 (our calculation); "vs. yr ago" in points. Potential weeks: average weeks of benefits new claimants are entitled to (DOL ETA 218, quarterly). Continuing-claims levels are not comparable across states: state benefit limits range from about 12 to 26 weeks, so in short-limit states UI claims end before unemployment does, and their continuing claims rates look low. Sources: DOL ETA 539, ETA 5159, ETA 218; BLS via FRED. The state files are on the Data & Downloads page.
